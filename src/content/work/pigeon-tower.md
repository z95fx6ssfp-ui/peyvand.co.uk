Pigeon Tower is a sound installation drawing on the pigeon towers of Esfahan, Iran: built for thousands of birds and now mostly standing empty. This one is wooden, built from CNC-cut plywood in a church hall in north London, with rows of arched niches inside. It is an impression of a building from a memory rather than a replica.

Over the summer of 2026 I ran sound workshops with community groups in Leicester and asked one question: what sounds do you keep and carry with you as a piece of "home", and what do you want to preserve and pass on? Rivers and streams came up again, even for people who grew up in cities. So did the rising call of the koel in Pakistan, the "wind of 120 days" in Sistan, now sold as white noise for sleeping, and the sound of Polish changing as it passes to Leicester-born children.

We sampled and layered those sounds together on a Fieldtone Weaver, a Box of Uncertainty and a KOMA Field Kit. In the tower, each recording is copied and re-copied until only a trace of the original is left. Generation loss as inheritance. Step inside, tune in and listen out for the fleeting moments.

It opened at LCB Depot, Leicester, for Journeys Festival International, 17 to 25 October 2026. Free, drop in, wheelchair accessible.
