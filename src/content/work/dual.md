Peyvand is British. Parisa is Iranian. One woman, two passports, two names.

DUAL دوگانه (do-gâ-neh) starts with a childhood visit to Iran with my dad, where we were held by the state and could not come home to the UK. It is not a story asking for pity. It is a satire on the officialdom of nationality: the forms, the queues, the rules nobody chose that shape a whole life.

Pedar (Father) leads the way through East London concrete and the old streets of Esfahan. There is puppetry, there is drag, and there is an audio-visual chanting and dancing mega-mix. The show was made before the Woman, Life, Freedom protests began, and since then it has asked a harder question of me: what my responsibilities are here, not only what Parisa's life is there.

It was developed with an international team, some of whom work anonymously for their own safety. The show comes with a digital companion zine with accessibility features and a tour pack for programmers. To talk about booking it, write to dual@peyvand.co.uk.
